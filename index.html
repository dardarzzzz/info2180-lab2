My First Weeks Learning C

September 29, 2026 by Darian Matthews

Starting C this semester has changed how I think about programming. Coming from higher-level languages, I'm used to the runtime quietly handling memory, strings, and types. In C, nothing is hidden, and every byte you use is a byte you are responsible for. That can be frustrating at first, but it makes the machine feel a lot more real.

Pointers were the biggest hurdle. It took a while before the idea of an address pointing to a value really clicked, but once it did, arrays, strings, and function arguments all started to make sense. I'm now writing small programs just to see what happens in memory, and it has been one of the most rewarding parts of my coursework so far.

Why Economics and Computer Science Fit Together

September 27, 2026 by Darian Matthews

Studying economics and computer science at the same time sounds like an odd pairing, but they overlap more than I expected. Both are about systems made of many small decisions, whether those are individual buyers and sellers in a market or individual instructions running on a processor. Understanding how the small pieces interact is the key to understanding the whole.

Microeconomics has also made me think differently about efficiency and trade-offs in code. Every design choice has a cost, whether it is time, memory, or complexity, and good engineering is a lot like good resource allocation. I'm looking forward to finding more places where these two fields inform each other.
